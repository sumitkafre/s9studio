Sales Playbook

One page you read before every call. Who we sell to, who we don't, what to ask, what to say when they push back, and what it costs.

Ideal client in one sentence

We work with [company type, e.g. funded B2B software companies] of [20–200] people who ship design every week but don't have, or can't hire fast enough, a senior in-house design team.

Good fit when they…
  • Have ongoing design work, not a single one-off project
  • Have a budget of at least [monthly fee of the entry plan] a month
  • Can name one decision-maker who will give feedback within 48 hours
  • Want a partner who joins their tools and rituals
  • Can start within [6] weeks
Not a fit when they…
  • Need one logo or one landing page and nothing after
  • Want fixed-price spec work or free pitch concepts
  • Have no one with authority to approve work
  • Shop on price alone or ask for a discount before scope
  • Expect us to replace a whole product team
Discovery questions
  1. Context. Tell me about the company and where design sits today. Who does it now?
  2. Problem. What made you start looking for design help now?
  3. Stakes. What happens if this stays the same for another six months?
  4. Success. Six months from now, what would make you say this worked?
  5. Work. What's on the design list for the next 90 days?
  6. People. Who would we work with day to day? Who signs off?
  7. Process. How do you usually choose a partner like us? Who else is involved?
  8. Budget. Do you have a monthly range in mind for design?
  9. Timing. When would you ideally want someone working on this?

Close every call with the two-minute explainer: "We work as your embedded design team on a monthly subscription. You get a dedicated senior designer in your tools, weekly shipping, and you can pause or stop with [30] days' notice. Most clients start with a one-week paid trial on a real problem."

Answers to common objections
They sayYou say
"It's expensive."Compare it with a senior hire: salary, recruiting, tools and three months to ramp. The trial week lets you check the value before committing.
"We could hire in-house."You could, and some clients do later. We cover the gap now and help you hire when you're ready.
"We tried an agency before."What went wrong? (Listen.) We work inside your tools on a weekly rhythm, and you can stop with [30] days' notice.
"Not the right time."Understood. What would need to change? Can I check in on [date]?
"Can you do a free sample?"We don't do free work, but the trial is one paid week on a real problem with clear success criteria.
"What about our IP?"Once paid, you own the final work. It's in the trial brief and the agreement.

A short two-way NDA for when a prospect asks for one or needs to share confidential material before the proposal.

Draft for discussion with your lawyer. If the prospect sends their own NDA, check it against these points instead of insisting on this one.

This Mutual Non-Disclosure Agreement is made on [date] between S9 Studio [legal entity, address] and [Company legal name, address] (each a "Party").

  1. Purpose. The Parties want to share information to discuss a possible design partnership (the "Purpose").
  2. Confidential information. Any non-public information one Party shares with the other, in any form, that is marked confidential or that a reasonable person would understand to be confidential.
  3. Exclusions. Information that is or becomes public through no fault of the receiving Party, was already known to it, is received from a third party without restriction, or is developed independently.
  4. Obligations. The receiving Party will use confidential information only for the Purpose, protect it with at least reasonable care, and share it only with employees and advisers who need it and are bound by similar duties.
  5. Required disclosure. If the law requires disclosure, the receiving Party will tell the other Party first where allowed and disclose only what is required.
  6. Return or destruction. On request, each Party will return or delete the other's confidential information, except copies kept in routine backups or required by law.
  7. No licence or obligation. No rights are granted other than to use information for the Purpose. Neither Party must enter into any further agreement.
  8. Term. This agreement covers information shared within [12] months of the date above. Obligations last [3] years from each disclosure.
  9. Remedies. A breach may cause harm that money cannot fix, so the disclosing Party may seek an injunction as well as other remedies.
  10. Law. This agreement is governed by the laws of [jurisdiction].

Delivery & QA checklist

How S9 works each week and what "done" means. Every piece of work passes this list before the client sees it.

The weekly cycle
WhenWhat happens
MondayPlan the week from the top of the queue. Confirm priorities in the client channel.
DailyWork in progress shared in the channel. Questions asked early, not on Friday.
WednesdayMid-week check: on track or flag the risk now.
ThursdayInternal review by a second designer or the Delivery Lead.
FridayShip. Send the weekly update email.
MonthlyMonthly Review deck: shipped, impact, next month's priorities.
Definition of done · the work
  • Answers the brief and the request as written
  • Checked by a second person
  • Uses the client's design system or brand rules
  • Text contrast meets WCAG AA
  • All states covered: empty, loading, error, long text
  • Works at every breakpoint that matters
  • No spelling mistakes or placeholder text
Definition of done · the handoff
  • Files named and organised in the client's workspace
  • Components and styles linked, not detached
  • Handoff notes for engineering or print
  • Assets exported in the formats needed
  • Third-party licences recorded
  • Request marked done in the queue
  • Included in Friday's weekly update

If a piece can't pass the list by Friday, ship what passes and say clearly in the update what moves to next week and why.
